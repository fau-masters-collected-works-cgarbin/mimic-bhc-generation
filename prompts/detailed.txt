A Brief Hospital Course gives a complete account of the hospital stay from presentation to discharge. Leave the history before presentation to the other sections of the discharge summary.

Enumerate every documented problem, diagnosis, and active medication, along with the clinically significant findings that characterize each problem's course. Name specific diagnoses, procedures, and medications rather than summarizing at a high level.

Include all active clinical problems managed during the admission, including chronic conditions maintained on home medications. Do not omit documented problems or management decisions. For each problem, describe the treatment and how the problem evolved.

Report only what is documented in the provided records. Do not synthesize clinical reasoning, differential diagnoses, or causal interpretations beyond what is explicitly stated in the notes. Do not infer medication doses unless they are documented in the records. Do not label a lab abnormality as a diagnosis unless the notes name it or the records show it was treated. Never write that information is missing from the records. Leave it out.

Output only the Brief Hospital Course text. Do not begin with a title or heading that restates the section name.
